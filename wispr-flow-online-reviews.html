I Dictate All My Reviews Now — and They Actually Get Read

8 October 2026 • Productivity

Honest confession: I used to be the person who had a great meal, a brilliant hotel stay, or a plumber who genuinely saved my week — and then never left a review. Not because I didn't care. Because the thought of tapping out three thoughtful paragraphs on my phone at 11pm made me close the tab every single time.

That changed when I started dictating my reviews with Wispr Flow.

How it works for me

Right after the experience — still in the restaurant, still in the hotel lobby, still standing in my fixed kitchen — I open the review page, hit dictate, and just talk. "The ramen here is unreal, the broth is rich without being heavy, service was fast even on a Friday night, only downside is it gets loud so don't come for a quiet date." Done. Thirty seconds. Wispr Flow cleans up the ums, fixes the punctuation, and what lands in the box reads like I actually sat down and wrote it.

Why it works so well

Reviews are opinion — and opinions live in your voice, not your fingertips. Typing flattens everything into "Great place, highly recommend." Speaking keeps the detail: the specific dish, the staff member's name, the thing that was slightly off. That's exactly the detail that makes a review useful — and it's why mine started picking up "helpful" votes almost immediately.

There's a selfish benefit too. Business owners respond to detailed reviews. I've had two restaurants reply personally and one hotel upgrade me on a return visit because they remembered the review. A generic five-star tap gets you nothing. A dictated review with specifics gets you remembered.

The numbers

Before: maybe 2 reviews a year, both one-liners. Since switching to dictation about two months ago: 23 reviews, averaging 80–120 words each, and most of them took under a minute. My Google Local Guide level went up twice. Total extra effort: basically zero, because I was already thinking the thoughts — I just say them now instead of losing them.

Try it this week

Next time you catch yourself thinking "that was really good" — don't wait until you're home. Open the review page, dictate it on the spot while the details are fresh, and post. One review. You'll feel the difference immediately.

If you haven't tried voice dictation yet, give Wispr Flow a go with my link — it's free to start, and it's the reason my reviews went from nonexistent to genuinely useful. Your favourite restaurant will thank you.
