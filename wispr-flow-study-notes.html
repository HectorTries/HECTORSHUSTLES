I Finally Remember What I Study — I Just Say It Out Loud

26 September 2026 • Productivity

I've started three online courses this year. Finished zero. Not because the material was hard, but because my notes were useless — half-typed bullet points I never looked at again, screenshots buried in my camera roll, and that familiar feeling of rewatching the same lesson three times.

Last weekend I tried something different. I put my laptop aside, opened a blank note with Wispr Flow running, and just talked through what I'd learned like I was explaining it to a friend.

"Okay, so the big idea from module four is that pricing is positioning. If you charge the lowest price you get the most price-sensitive customers, which means the most support work for the least money. So instead you anchor high, then justify it with one specific outcome."

Two minutes of rambling. Wispr Flow turned it into clean paragraphs with the filler stripped out. I read it back, added one line at the top — "Pricing = positioning, anchor high, justify with outcome" — and moved on to the next lesson.

I did five of these little voice recaps in about 20 minutes. By the end I had a full page of study notes that actually sounded like me, which means I actually understood them. That's the test nobody talks about: if you can't explain it out loud without reading, you don't know it yet.

The best part came two days later. Before starting module five, I reread my dictated notes instead of rewatching module four. Five minutes and I was back up to speed. Normally I'd have lost an hour rewatching videos at 1.5x speed and still feeling foggy.

Here's what I learned: typing notes keeps you in copy mode. You're transcribing slides, not thinking. Talking forces you to process — you pause, you rephrase, you catch the bit you didn't get. The note becomes proof you learned something, not proof you were present.

It works for anything you're learning. Course videos, a how-to book, even a YouTube tutorial on fixing the boiler. Watch a chunk, pause, explain it back out loud, keep the transcript.

If you're sitting on half-finished courses like I was, try this: after your next lesson, dictate a two-minute recap before you open another tab. You'll know instantly what stuck and what didn't.
