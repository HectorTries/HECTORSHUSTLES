I Planned Thanksgiving Dinner in 18 Minutes — By Talking It Out on a Walk

21 October 2026 • Productivity

I volunteered to host Thanksgiving this year. Then I remembered what that actually means.

Twelve people. One turkey. Three dietary requirements I keep forgetting. My mum's stuffing expectations. My partner's vegan sister. A cousin who "doesn't do Brussels sprouts." And a group chat already asking what time to arrive.

Every year I do the same thing: open a blank note, type "Thanksgiving plan," and freeze. The list lives in my head as a stressy swirl — turkey size, sides, shopping, timings, who brings what. Typing it feels like doing admin. So I put it off until the week before, then panic-shop twice.

This year, on a Sunday walk, I talked it out instead.

How it works for me

I opened Wispr Flow and dictated the whole dinner like I was explaining it to a friend. Twelve guests. Turkey for twelve plus leftovers. The must-haves: roast turkey, mum's stuffing, roast potatoes, gravy, two veg, one vegan main for my sister-in-law, apple pie and ice cream for dessert.

Two minutes covered the menu. Another three covered logistics — drinks, dessert, arrival time, when the turkey goes in.

That gave me about 400 words of raw plan — messy but complete. The line that made it work: "The turkey goes in at 11 for a 3pm sit-down, everything else works backwards from there."

I spent ten minutes trimming the ramble into three lists: buy, cook, delegate. Shopping list, cook-day timeline, and three texts to family with their assignments. Whole thing done before I got home.

Why it works so well

Thanksgiving planning is thinking out loud, and thinking comes out better spoken. When I type, I try to make a perfect list and stall on the first line. When I talk, I just narrate the day — morning, turkey in, guests arrive, table, dessert — and the list builds itself.

Speaking also catches the details typing misses. Talking it through, I remembered the gravy boat, the extra chairs, the oat milk for my sister-in-law's coffee. The small stuff that ruins the day when you forget it.

The numbers

Old approach: three evenings of dread, two panic shops, one forgotten side dish, £40 of duplicate buys. New approach: five minutes of talking, thirteen minutes of trimming, one clean shopping list, one timeline on the fridge, three delegation texts sent. Zero swirl, and I actually feel ready a month out.

Try it today

Hosting anything this season? Open a voice note, hit dictate, and start with "Here's who's coming and what we're eating…" Talk for three minutes — guests, menu, timings, who brings what. Then trim it into buy, cook, delegate.
