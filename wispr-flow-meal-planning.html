I Dictate My Meal Plan Every Sunday — It Takes 4 Minutes

9 September 2026 • Productivity

Sunday used to mean 20 minutes staring into the fridge, another 15 scribbling a half-legible shopping list, and one inevitable mid-week "we forgot the onions" trip.

Now? I talk to my phone for four minutes and the whole week is sorted.

The Sunday ritual

I open the fridge, open the cupboards, and just… talk. "Monday, chicken stir-fry, need peppers and soy sauce. Tuesday, pasta bake, need mince and cheese. Wednesday, leftovers. Thursday…" Wispr Flow catches all of it — messy, mid-thought, going back to correct myself — and hands me a clean, organised plan.

The magic is that I'm looking at the shelves while I talk. I see the half-bag of rice. I notice we're low on olive oil. Try doing that while typing on a phone with one hand and holding a jar of pesto in the other. Dictation wins every time.

Why this works so well

Speed. Talking is roughly 3x faster than typing. A full week of meals plus a shopping list in under five minutes, no exaggeration.

Hands-free. You're in the kitchen. Your hands are busy, possibly covered in flour. Voice is the only input method that makes sense there.

The "oh wait" factor. Meal planning is full of mid-sentence corrections — "actually let's do tacos Friday instead." Typed lists make backtracking painful. Spoken planning handles it naturally, and Wispr's AI cleans up the mess so the final text reads like you meant it the first time.

My actual workflow

  • Sunday, fridge open, mic on. Dictate 5–7 dinners, noting ingredients I already have vs. need.
  • Wispr formats it. I get a neat plan + a grouped shopping list (produce, dairy, meat, pantry).
  • Screenshot the list, shop once. One trip. No forgotten onions.

The result: I spend maybe 4 minutes planning, save a mid-week shop (~30 min), waste less food, and — honestly — eat better because the decision is already made when 6pm rolls around.

The takeaway

The best productivity hacks aren't apps with dashboards. They're removing the friction from things you already do. I was always going to stand in front of that fridge on Sunday. Now I just talk while I do it.
