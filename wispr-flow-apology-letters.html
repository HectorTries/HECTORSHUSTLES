I Ruined a Friendship Over Text — One Dictated Apology Fixed It That Evening

16 October 2026 • Productivity

I sent a stupid message in a group chat last month. It was meant as a joke about my mate cancelling our weekend trip, but it landed wrong — sarcastic, pointed, the kind of thing that reads ten times worse than it sounds. He went quiet. Two days of one-word replies. I'd managed to turn six years of friendship into an awkward customer-service exchange.

I typed five versions of an apology. Every one sounded either grovelling or defensive. "I'm sorry IF you were offended…" Delete. "Look, you know I didn't mean it like that…" Delete. Typing made me lawyer every sentence instead of just saying I was wrong.

So that evening, walking home from the station, I stopped typing and started talking.

How it works for me

I opened Wispr Flow and gave myself one rule: say it like he's standing in front of me. I talked for four minutes straight — what I actually meant by the joke, why I could see it stung, that cancelling the trip genuinely disappointed me and I handled it badly.

That ramble gave me about 300 words of raw material — messy, honest, in my voice. One sentence wrote itself: "You bailed on the trip and instead of saying I was gutted, I tried to be funny about it, and I was just hurtful."

At home I spent ten minutes trimming it: own it first, no "if" or "but", then stop. Sent it under 150 words. He replied within the hour. Pint on Thursday. Sorted.

Why it works so well

Apologies are spoken, so typing them first starts in the wrong medium. Typing makes you negotiate with yourself. Talking forces you to hear whether you actually sound sorry — your voice catches the hedging that your fingers let through.

Speaking also surfaces the real reason, not the safe one. I'd never have typed "I was gutted you cancelled." But I said it out loud because it was true, and that's the line he quoted back when he forgave me.

The numbers

Old approach: five typed drafts over two days, zero sent, one friendship freezing over. New approach: four minutes of talking, ten minutes of trimming, one message sent that evening. Reply within the hour, pint within the week.

Try it tonight

Owe someone a sorry? Open a voice note, hit dictate, and finish the sentence "What I actually felt was…" Keep talking for three minutes. Don't edit, don't restart. Cut the "ifs" and "buts" after, keep the honest bit in the middle, and send it.

If a blank page is turning your apology into a legal defence, give Wispr Flow a go with my link — it's free to start, and it turned two days of dread into a four-minute walk home. Say it like you mean it.
