One voice memo, three pieces of content (in the time it takes to make tea)

23 August 2026 • Productivity

Use case: Turning a single dictated idea into a TikTok script, a Medium draft, and a tweet thread — without sitting down to "write".

I used to lose ideas at the speed I had them. I'd be walking the dog, a genuinely good story would hit me — the one about the mattress king who built a billion-dollar empire on a handshake — and by the time I got home it was gone, replaced by whatever was in my inbox.

The fix wasn't more discipline. It was talking to my phone.

Now the moment an idea lands, I hit record and just talk. Two minutes, rambling, zero structure — that's fine. Wispr Flow turns it into clean text while I'm still walking. Then the magic happens: that one dictation becomes my entire content pipeline.

1. The TikTok script comes first. I paste the transcription into my notes, and Wispr helps me tighten it into a 60-second story — hook, stakes, payoff. The ramble becomes a script with a beginning, a middle, and a punchline. For "Fortune and Folly," that's the whole job: one wild business story, told fast.

2. The Medium draft writes itself. Same dictation, different frame. What was a punchy video script gets expanded into a proper article — the background, the numbers, the lesson. Because the raw material is already there in my words, the draft keeps my voice. It doesn't read like AI mush; it reads like me, just with better grammar.

3. The thread is the leftovers. The best lines from the script become a 4-tweet thread. Ten minutes, done.

That's three pieces of content from one two-minute walk, and I never once stared at a blank page. The blank page is the real enemy — not the writing. Dictation removes the blank page entirely because you're not writing, you're talking, and talking is easy.

If you create anything — videos, articles, newsletters, threads — stop typing your first drafts. Dictate them. Let the tool do the typing while you do the thinking.
