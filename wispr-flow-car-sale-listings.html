My Car Sat Unsold for 6 Weeks — I Rewrote the Listing by Voice and Sold It in 4 Days

29 October 2026 • Productivity

My old Focus sat on Facebook Marketplace for six weeks. Plenty of views, zero serious buyers. The listing was three lazy sentences I had typed at midnight: "Good car, runs well, MOT till March." No details, no story, nothing that made anyone picture themselves driving it.

I knew the listing was the problem. I just could not face rewriting it. Sitting down to type a proper advert felt like homework I had been avoiding since school.

Then I tried something different. I walked around the car with Wispr Flow open and just talked about it.

How it went

Ten minutes of walking and talking. "2017 Ford Focus, 1.5 diesel, 68,000 miles. Full service history, every stamp at the same garage. New tyres all round last year. MOT till March with zero advisories. Heated seats, parking sensors, Bluetooth. One tiny scratch on the rear bumper from a car park, honestly barely visible. Selling because we got a bigger car for the kids. Drives perfectly, never let me down once."

By the time I got back inside, I had a full listing. I pasted the same advert to Marketplace, AutoTrader, and Gumtree in minutes — before, each one needed a separate typing session I would never get round to.

Specifics build trust. Vague listings scream "what is he hiding?" Saying it aloud while looking at the car meant nothing got missed — the service stamps, the new tyres, the honest little scratch.

Why talking beats typing here

A car advert fails when it is vague. Typing at a desk, you write "runs well" and call it done because finding the mileage and service details means digging through paperwork.

Walking around the car and narrating what you see flips that. You spot the heated seats, you remember the new tyres, you mention the scratch honestly because you are looking right at it. The details come to you instead of you hunting for them.

It also killed the dread. A proper listing felt like an hour of typing in my head. Talking through it showed me I already knew every selling point — I just needed to say each one once while standing next to the car.

The result

Three viewings booked that weekend, sold on Tuesday for £400 more than my lowest "just get rid of it" price. Enquiries went from tyre-kickers asking "what is the mileage?" to serious buyers asking when they could view — the listing answered everything upfront.

The buyer said the advert was the best he had seen — clear, honest, detailed. Old me would have kept the rubbish listing up for another month and accepted a lowball offer out of frustration.

Try it before your next listing

Got a car to sell? Don't start by staring at a blank listing form. Walk around it, say what you see, and let the advert write itself. Hands free, eyes on the car, nothing left out.
