I Wrote My Wedding Vows by Talking Out Loud — and Didn't Cry Until the Day

14 October 2026 • Productivity

I'd been engaged for eight months and hadn't written a single word of my vows. Every time I sat down to type them, I sounded like a greeting card. "My dearest darling" — who even talks like that? Not me. I deleted more drafts than I care to admit.

Two weeks before the wedding I gave up typing entirely. I went for a walk, put my earbuds in, and just talked. What came out made my fiancée cry when she finally heard it — in a good way.

How it works for me

I opened Wispr Flow on my phone and started with the simplest prompt I could think of: "Tell the story of us." No structure, no pressure. I talked about our first date (I was 20 minutes late), the flat we nearly didn't get, the Sunday mornings that matter more to me than any big holiday.

Four minutes of rambling gave me about 300 words of raw material — messy, honest, completely in my voice. There was a bit where I said "you make bad days feel like they've got an off switch" and I knew instantly that was staying in.

Then I sat down for ten minutes and shaped it: one memory to open, two promises in the middle, one line to close. Done. Total time: under fifteen minutes, most of it walking.

Why it works so well

Vows are spoken words, so writing them by typing is starting in the wrong medium. When you type, you reach for formal language that doesn't sound like you. When you talk, you sound like yourself — because you are yourself.

Speaking also surfaces the details you'd never type. I would never have sat down and typed "the way you steal chips off my plate before your own food arrives." But I said it out loud because it was true, and it got the biggest laugh of the ceremony.

The numbers

Old approach: eight months of avoidance, six deleted drafts, one rising sense of panic. New approach: fifteen minutes total, one set of vows, zero greeting-card clichés. Two guests asked me afterwards who helped me write them. Nobody believed I did it on a walk.

Try it tonight

Even if your wedding is months away, try this: open a voice note, hit dictate, and finish the sentence "The moment I knew was…" Keep talking for three minutes. Don't edit, don't restart. You'll have the raw material for vows that actually sound like you.
