I Do Morning Pages by Talking — 10 Minutes That Clear My Head

28 September 2026 • Productivity

For years I tried journaling the 'right' way. Sit down, open a blank page, write three longhand pages before checking my phone. I lasted four days. My hand cramped, my brain censored every sentence, and the whole thing felt like homework before the day had even started.

Then I tried it with voice instead. Same idea — dump what's in your head first thing — but I just talk while the kettle boils.

"Okay. Woke up groggy, slept badly again, kept scrolling too late. Big thing today is the dentist at two, don't forget the invoice after. Feeling anxious about the money stuff but honestly it's just the Vinted payout being slow. Also — idea — film the morning-pages thing itself, people love routines. Call mum back. That's it."

Ninety seconds of rambling. Wispr Flow turns it into clean paragraphs — no ums, punctuation in the right places, 'two' instead of 'to'. I glance at it while the tea brews, and my head feels noticeably quieter.

Here's why talking beats typing for this: typing makes me perform. I start editing, making sentences pretty, skipping the embarrassing bits. Talking skips all that. I hear myself say 'I'm anxious about money' and it's just true — I don't dress it up. The transcript catches the honest version before my inner editor wakes up.

I've done it most mornings for two weeks now. Total time: about ten minutes including re-reading. Cost: zero willpower, because I'm just muttering into my phone while doing something I'd do anyway.

The results surprised me. First, I stop carrying vague worries around. Once 'call mum back' is on the page, my brain stops looping it. Second, I catch patterns. Reading back a week of pages, I noticed 'slept badly / scrolled too late' showed up four times. That stung enough that I started charging my phone in the kitchen. Sleep's already better.

Third — and this is the one I didn't expect — it's a goldmine for content. Half my post ideas now come straight from morning rambles. Real thoughts, in my real voice, captured before I've had time to sound like everyone else.

If your mornings feel noisy, try this tomorrow: while the kettle boils, open a note, turn on dictation, and say everything on your mind. Don't edit, don't filter. Just empty the tank.
