From Voice Memo to Published Post: Writing Content in the Cracks of the Day

3 September 2026 • Productivity

September 3, 2026 — draft for Medium

Every content creator knows the feeling: the idea is brilliant at 8am and gone by 8pm. The gap between "that would make a great post" and "okay, time to sit down and write it" is where most content dies — because the sitting down never happens.

I stopped trying to close that gap. I started talking instead.

Capture the idea while it's still hot

The trick isn't writing more. It's capturing more, at the moment the thought exists. For me that's usually not at a desk — it's walking, driving, cooking, or in that weird five-minute pocket between meetings.

So I tap the mic and talk at the idea like it's a person:

"Story idea: the mattress king who sold more sofas than anyone by financing them at zero percent — and why 'customers who can't afford it' turned out to be his best customers. Hook: everyone said he was crazy. Punchline: he understood cash flow better than the banks."

Wispr Flow turns that rambling into clean text — punctuation, structure, everything. What used to be a scribbled line in a notes app is now a usable opening paragraph.

The first draft is already done

Here's the part that changed my output: when I capture an idea properly, the voice memo is the first draft. It has the tone, the pacing, the way I'd actually tell the story. When I finally open the laptop, I'm not staring at a blank page — I'm polishing something that already sounds like me.

The edit takes fifteen minutes instead of the two hours of false starts a blank page usually costs. Posts that used to be "someday" become "this week."

Batch the boring writing, too

Newsletters, email replies, captions, the little intros and outros — they all get the same treatment. I dictate them in one go while doing something else, then do one quick pass for typos. The admin writing stops eating the creative hours.

Why it works

Because creative work doesn't fit into tidy calendar blocks. It lives in the cracks — and voice is the only tool I've found that makes the cracks usable. You're not "finding time to write." You're talking about the thing you already think about all day anyway.
