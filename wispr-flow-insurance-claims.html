My Phone Screen Smashed on Saturday — I Dictated the Insurance Claim in 12 Minutes and Got Paid in 4 Days

26 October 2026 • Productivity

Insurance claim forms are where motivation goes to die. Tiny boxes, fiddly dates, and that one question — "describe the incident in detail" — staring at you while you try to remember what day it even happened.

My phone slipped off a café table last Saturday. Face down on tile, screen spiderwebbed. Old me would've left the claim form open in a tab for two weeks and paid out of pocket out of sheer inertia.

This time I opened Wispr Flow and just talked it out.

How it went

I sat on the bus home and dictated the whole incident while it was fresh: where I was, what time, how the phone fell, the damage I could see, the case model and policy number from the email in my inbox. About four minutes of talking, facts first.

Then I kept going and turned it into the written statement. "On Saturday 24 October at around 2pm, my phone fell from a table onto a tiled floor at the café on High Street. The front screen cracked across the full display. The phone still powers on. I am claiming for screen repair under policy number X."

Saying it aloud stopped me overthinking. Typed, I tie myself in knots trying to sound official. Spoken, it came out plain and factual — exactly what an assessor wants.

I pasted the text into the online form, uploaded two photos of the damage, and submitted. Twelve minutes door to door.

Why talking beats typing here

Claims live or die on specifics: dates, times, what broke, what you did next. Typing from memory a week later, you hedge everything — "I think it was around…" — and hedged claims get queried back.

Dictating the same day meant I narrated what had literally just happened. No hedging, no gaps. Exact time, exact place, exact damage.

It also killed the dread. The form felt like a two-hour job in my head. Talking through it showed me I already knew every answer — I just needed to say them once.

The result

Claim approved Tuesday. £180 payout landed Thursday — four days after the drop. One submission, zero follow-up questions, no back-and-forth asking for the policy number or a clearer description.

The repair shop fixed the screen the same weekend. A good claim tells them everything they need to approve it in one read.

Try it next time something breaks

Cracked screen, dented bumper, delayed bag — don't let the form sit in a tab for a fortnight. Open Wispr Flow, say what happened while it's fresh, and paste the result into the claim. Photos plus a clear dictated statement beat a perfect typed essay every time.
