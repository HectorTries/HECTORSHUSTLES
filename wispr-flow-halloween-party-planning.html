I Planned a Halloween Party in 5 Minutes — By Talking Out Loud

6 October 2026 • Productivity

Halloween is three weeks away and I'd agreed to host. Eight people, my flat, Saturday the 31st. Then the group chat started: "what's the food situation?", "costumes mandatory?", "can I bring my cousin?" Suddenly I was staring at a blank notes app with that familiar dread — guest list, snacks, drinks, decorations, playlist, cleaning — all swirling around with nothing written down.

Last year I planned a birthday the typed way. Forty minutes, three rewritten lists, and I still forgot ice. This time I did it differently. I put the kettle on, opened Wispr Flow, and just talked.

The 5-minute party download

Here's roughly what I said: "Halloween party, Saturday 31st, eight people plus maybe Tom's cousin. Food — pizza bases and toppings so people build their own, plus crisps and that pumpkin dip. Drinks — beer, a big batch cocktail, apple juice for the non-drinkers. Decorations — fairy lights, paper bats, the plastic skeleton from last year. Costumes mandatory, best dressed gets a prize, a bottle of something. Playlist — Halloween mix plus normal stuff after midnight. Cleaning — hallway and bathroom Friday night, living room Saturday morning. Oh and message the group with arrival time, seven-ish, and tell them to bring a pumpkin if they want the carving contest."

Rambling. Out of order. Half plan, half remembering things mid-sentence. Exactly how party planning actually sounds in your head.

Wispr Flow turned it into a clean plan — food and drinks grouped, a to-buy list separated from a to-do list, the messages I owe people pulled out at the bottom. I glanced at it once and the party had a shape.

Why voice wins for party planning

Party plans don't arrive organised. You think "drinks" and remember your mate doesn't drink, which reminds you about the cocktail, which reminds you someone needs a costume prize. Typing forces you to file each thought before you've finished thinking it, so half of them escape.

Talking catches the chain. I only remembered the carving contest because saying "bring a pumpkin" out loud sparked it — a typed list would never have surfaced that. And hearing the list back showed me I'd overplanned food for eight people, so I cut two dishes in seconds instead of rewriting a list.

The result

Five minutes, one talking session, party planned. Shopping list done, cleaning split across two days, group message sent the same evening. No forgotten ice this time — because the list was built from how I actually think, not how a template thinks I should.

If you're hosting anything this Halloween, try saying the plan out loud before you type a word. Dump it all, glance at the list, adjust once — then go enjoy your own party.
