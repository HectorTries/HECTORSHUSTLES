I Rehearsed My Salary Negotiation Out Loud — and Walked Away With £4,000 More

24 October 2026 • Productivity

I used to bomb salary negotiations. Not because I didn't deserve more — because I'd freeze the second money came up, mumble something about "being flexible," and accept the first number on the table.

Last month I finally fixed it. I rehearsed the whole negotiation out loud, on a walk, using Wispr Flow.

How it went

I had a job offer sitting in my inbox. Good role, decent base — but £3,000 below what I'd told myself I wanted. Old me would've just clicked "accept" and resented it for a year.

Instead, I opened Wispr Flow and started talking. I dictated everything I knew: the offer number, my target range, my competing evidence — two years of results, the market rate I'd researched, the extra responsibilities in the job spec that weren't in my current role.

About ten minutes of talking. Messy, rambling, half-sentences. But it forced me to say the scary number out loud: "I'm looking for £4,000 above their offer, and here's why."

Then I did something I'd never done before. I dictated my actual counter-email, word by word, while walking. Hearing myself say "based on my experience and the market rate" stopped it sounding arrogant in my head. It just sounded… reasonable.

Why talking beats typing here

Negotiation emails are emotional. When I type them, I either go too soft ("sorry to ask, totally understand if not possible!!") or overcorrect into robotic corporate-speak.

Speaking hits the middle. I could hear when I sounded apologetic and rephrase on the spot. I caught myself underselling twice — "I think maybe I could possibly…" became "my track record shows…" just by hearing how weak it sounded.

I ran through my three key points three times until they felt natural. By the third pass, I wasn't reading a script. I actually believed my own case.

The numbers

Sent the email that evening — twelve minutes of trimming the dictated draft into a tight, confident counter. Three short paragraphs: gratitude, evidence, clear ask.

They came back two days later. Not the full ask, but £4,000 above the original offer, plus an extra day of annual leave I hadn't even requested. Total time invested: about 25 minutes on a walk I'd have taken anyway.

That's roughly £160 per minute. Best-paid walk of my life.

Try it today

Got a review, offer, or raise conversation coming up? Don't type your talking points into a doc you'll never re-read. Open Wispr Flow, hit dictate, and say your number out loud. Argue your own case while you walk. You'll hear the weak spots instantly — and fix them before they cost you money.
