My Kitchen Reno Quotes Were a Mess — I Dictated the Whole Plan in 20 Minutes and Saved £800

28 October 2026 • Productivity

Getting renovation quotes used to mean chaos. Three builders, three different WhatsApp threads, one with "kitchen, full job, £4k mate" and nothing else. I had measurements on a scrap of paper, inspiration screenshots buried in my camera roll, and no way to compare anything fairly.

Last month I finally tackled the kitchen properly. I did the whole brief by talking.

I stood in the kitchen with Wispr Flow open and just narrated what I wanted: rip out the old units, keep the boiler where it is, move the dishwasher next to the sink, new worktop, tile the splashback, repaint the ceiling, LED strips under the cabinets. Twenty minutes of walking around saying what stays, what goes, and what needs fixing.

How it went

I went job by job and said it aloud while looking at it. "Rip out old units. Keep boiler in place. Dishwasher moves next to sink. New worktop, tile splashback, repaint ceiling, LED strips under cabinets. Fix cracked grout behind hob, move the dead socket, replace the cabinet door that never closes." Measurements read straight off the tape, no typing.

Then I sent the same clean spec to all three builders — scope, materials, measurements, timeline. For the first time ever, the quotes came back directly comparable instead of three vague guesses.

Saying it in the room caught everything I would have missed at a desk. Typed briefs go stale the second you forget a corner. Spoken on the spot, nothing slipped.

Why talking beats typing here

Renovations live or die on the brief. Typing it from memory the night before, you miss half the room — then you are paying extra for "unforeseen" work that was staring at you all along.

Dictating while standing in the kitchen meant the plan matched reality. I saw it, I said it, it went on the spec. No gaps, no guessing.

It also killed the dread. A renovation brief felt like two weekends of stressful typing in my head. Talking through it showed me I already knew every job — I just needed to say each one once while looking at it.

The result

One clean plan, three comparable quotes, £800 saved. One quote was £800 higher for the exact same work — because the brief was identical, I could point at the line items and ask why. They dropped the price on the spot.

One builder said it was the clearest brief he had all year. Work starts next month at the agreed price, everything in writing because I said it once while looking at it.

Try it before your next project

Planning any home work? Don't start with a blank spreadsheet. Stand in the room, say what you see, and let the plan build itself. Hands free, eyes on the job, nothing left out.
