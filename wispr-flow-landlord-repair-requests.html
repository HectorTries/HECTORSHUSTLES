My Boiler Died on a Sunday — I Dictated the Landlord Email in 8 Minutes and Got It Fixed by Tuesday

25 October 2026 • Productivity

I hate emailing my landlord. Not because he's bad — because every repair request I type comes out either too apologetic or too aggressive, and then I sit on the draft for three days while the problem gets worse.

Last Sunday my boiler quit. No hot water, radiators stone cold, and a blinking error code I didn't understand. Old me would've typed half a message, worried I sounded demanding, and left it until Wednesday.

This time I opened Wispr Flow and just talked.

How it went

I stood in front of the boiler and dictated everything I could see: the error code, when it started, what I'd already tried — reset button, pressure check, thermostat batteries. About three minutes of rambling.

Then I kept talking and turned it into the actual message. "Hi, hope you're well, boiler's gone, here's the error code, no hot water since this morning, I've tried X and Y, could we get an engineer out early this week? I'm home Tuesday if that helps."

Saying it out loud kept me honest. I heard myself start with "so sorry to bother you AGAIN" and cut it on the spot.

I pasted the dictated text into an email, added two photos, and hit send. Total time: eight minutes, most of it standing in the kitchen.

Why talking beats typing here

Repair emails need facts, not feelings. When I type them, I either bury the key detail (the error code) under four paragraphs of justification, or I fire off something terse that reads like a threat.

Speaking fixed both. I described what was in front of me like I was showing a friend. Error code first, timeline second, what I need third. Clear and polite.

I also caught the tone before he did. Hearing my own voice made it obvious when I sounded annoyed, so I rephrased before sending.

The result

He replied within the hour. Engineer booked for Tuesday morning. Fixed in forty minutes — faulty pressure sensor, covered under his maintenance contract.

No chasing, no second email, no three cold showers. One clear message with the right details the first time meant no back-and-forth asking for the model number or when I was home.

A good repair request gives them everything they need to say yes in one go.

Try it next time something breaks

Got a leak, a broken appliance, or damp you've been putting off reporting? Don't stare at a blank email. Open Wispr Flow, stand in front of the problem, and describe what you see. Photos plus a clear dictated note beats a perfect typed letter every time.
