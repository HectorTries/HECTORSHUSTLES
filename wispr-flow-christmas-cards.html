I Wrote 25 Christmas Cards Without the Hand-Cramp Dread — I Dictated Them on a Walk

20 October 2026 • Productivity

I love sending Christmas cards. I hate writing them.

Every October I buy the box of 25, full of good intentions. Every December I stare at a blank pile, pen in hand, writing the same flat line over and over: "Merry Christmas, hope you're well!" By card six my hand aches. By card ten I sound like a robot. Last year twelve never got sent.

They feel forced because I'm trying to type with a pen — formal, careful, generic. Nothing like how I actually talk to these people.

So this year, on a Sunday walk, I talked them out instead.

How it works for me

I opened Wispr Flow and dictated each card like I was leaving a voice note. For my aunt: the summer barbecue, her new grandson, how I still owe her for the lemon cake recipe. Two minutes of talking per person, names and memories included.

That gave me about 100 words of warm material each — specific and personal. The line that made them work: "Thinking of you when I passed your street last week."

I spent two minutes per card trimming the ramble into three or four handwritten lines — one memory, one update, one wish. Twenty-five done in an afternoon, then I just copied them into the cards that evening.

Why it works so well

Christmas cards are spoken warmth, and warmth comes out better spoken. When I write cold, I default to Hallmark generic. When I talk, I remember the actual stuff — the joke, the visit, the thing they told me in July.

Speaking also beats the blank-card freeze. Talking it through out loud, I naturally included something real for each person — the detail that makes them keep the card on the mantel instead of recycling it on Boxing Day.

The numbers

Old approach: three evenings, aching hand, twelve cards sent, thirteen guilty leftovers. New approach: fifty minutes of talking on a walk, fifty minutes of trimming and copying, twenty-five personal cards posted before November. Zero dread, and my aunt rang me to say mine made her cry.

Try it today

Got cards, thank-yous, or festive messages you've been avoiding? Open a voice note, hit dictate, and start with "What I remember about them this year is…" Talk for two minutes — the memory, your news, your wish. Then trim it to three lines that fit the card.

If blank-page dread kills your good intentions every December, give Wispr Flow a go with my link — it's free to start, and it turned my Christmas pile from chore into highlight. Say it warm, write it fast.
