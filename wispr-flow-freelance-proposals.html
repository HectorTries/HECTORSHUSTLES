I Wrote a Freelance Proposal in 12 Minutes — And Won the Project

8 October 2026 • Productivity

A warm lead landed in my inbox on Monday morning. Newsletter setup for a small business, exactly the kind of work I want. In the past that email would have sat in drafts for two days while I agonised over every line.

My old proposals took two hours minimum. I'd start from a stiff template, rewrite the opening sentence ten times, and end up sounding like a robot in a suit. Desperate to impress, impossible to trust.

This time I did what I do now for anything I dread typing. I opened Wispr Flow and talked it out while making coffee.

The 12-minute talking proposal

Here's roughly what I said: "Okay, so what do they actually need. They've got two thousand subscribers but they only email once every few months, so engagement is dead. I'd move them to a simple weekly rhythm — one useful email, same day, same tone. First I'd clean the list and set up a welcome sequence, three emails, story plus offer. Then a basic template so it looks sharp without hours of design. I've done this three times before — last client went from a fifteen percent open rate to forty-one percent in six weeks. I can do the setup in a week, fixed price, then hand it over with a Loom walkthrough so they're not stuck paying me forever."

Rambling, but every line a client wants to hear was in there — diagnosis, plan, proof, timeline.

Wispr Flow turned it into clean text with the numbers intact. Ten minutes of tidying into short paragraphs, and I hit send.

Why voice wins for proposals

Proposals fail when they sound written instead of said. Clients skim dozens of "I am a highly motivated freelancer" pitches — what stands out is a human who actually understood the problem. But typing makes you perform. Formal words, hedged claims, the honest sentence deleted.

Talking skips the performance. Saying "your engagement is dead because you only email every few months" out loud feels direct and helpful. Typing it feels rude, so people water it down into nothing. The ramble also surfaces proof — I'd almost forgotten the fifteen to forty-one percent result until I heard myself say it, and that's the line that sells.

The result

Twelve minutes, one talking session, proposal sent before my coffee went cold. The client replied that afternoon: "This is the first proposal that actually got what we need." Project won, fixed fee, start Monday.

Got a lead sitting in drafts right now? Talk your way through it before typing a word. Say what's broken, say how you'd fix it, say what you've done before — then tidy and send.
