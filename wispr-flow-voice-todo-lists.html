I Plan My Whole Day in 90 Seconds — By Talking to My Laptop

19 September 2026 • Productivity

I used to start every morning staring at a blank to-do list, trying to remember what actually matters today.

Now I just talk. Coffee in hand, I tap Wispr Flow and ramble for ninety seconds.

"Okay. Today. Finish the Medium draft first, that's priority one. Reply to Sarah about Thursday, cancel the dentist — actually move it to next week. Gym at lunch, legs. Pick up milk and coffee filters. Oh, and invoice the podcast client before five, that's two hundred quid I'm forgetting about."

Messy. Half-sentences. No structure. Wispr Flow turns it into a clean list — priorities at the top, errands grouped, the little details kept.

Here's why this works so well: morning brain is foggy but honest. The moment you try to type a perfect list, you start editing yourself. You write "work on project" instead of what you really mean, which is "send the awkward follow-up email I've been avoiding." Talking skips the filter. You say what's actually on your mind, in the order it worries you — and that's usually the right priority order.

I've started doing it for everything planning-related. Sunday night brain-dump for the week ahead — "kids swimming Tuesday, car MOT Thursday, mum's birthday present still not ordered." End-of-day shutdown — "tomorrow: dentist forms, fix the porch light, call the bank." It takes a minute, and I sleep better because it's out of my head and somewhere I trust.

The numbers tell the story. Typing a proper daily plan used to take me ten to fifteen minutes of fiddling with apps and reordering bullets. Dictating it takes under two minutes, still half-asleep, coffee not even finished. That's over an hour a week back — and more importantly, I actually do the list, because it sounds like me, not like a productivity template.

If your mornings feel scattered, do yourself a favour: tomorrow, don't type. Just say your day out loud and let it sort itself.
