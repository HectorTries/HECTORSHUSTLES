I Rewrote My Dating Profile by Talking to Myself — and Got 3x More Matches

12 October 2026 • Productivity

Let me be honest: my old dating profile was terrible. "Love travel, good food, and Sunday lie-ins. Looking for someone who can make me laugh." I could have been anyone. It read like it was written by a committee — because in a way, it was: me, my overthinking brain, and three friends giving contradictory advice over WhatsApp.

Last month I deleted the whole thing and dictated a new one in ten minutes. Matches tripled within two weeks. Same photos. Same guy. Completely different words.

How it works for me

I opened a blank note, hit dictate on Wispr Flow, and answered one question out loud: "What would I actually say about myself on a good first date?" Not the polished version — the real one. I talked about the pasta recipe I've been perfecting for two years, the hiking trip where I got spectacularly lost, why I'll defend pineapple on pizza to my grave.

Five minutes of rambling gave me 400 words of raw material — specific, funny, unmistakably me. Then I spent five minutes cutting it down to a tight 150-word profile. The stories did the heavy lifting; I just trimmed the edges.

Why it works so well

Dating profiles are hard to type because typing makes you perform. Every sentence gets filtered through "will this impress a stranger?" and the result is the same generic mush everyone writes: travel, food, laughter, adventures. Nobody swipes right on "adventures."

Talking bypasses the performance. When you speak, you tell stories — the weirdly specific details that make you a person instead of a template. My new profile mentions the lost hiking trip and the pineapple hill I'll die on. Three different matches opened with "okay, I need to hear about the hiking trip." My old profile never got an opener like that. It barely got openers at all.

The numbers

Old profile: ~2 matches a week, mostly fizzling after "hey, how's your week going?" New profile: 6-7 matches a week, and the conversations start with something real because I gave them something real to react to. Ten minutes of talking beat six months of tweaking one bland sentence at a time.

Try it tonight

Open a blank note, hit dictate, and describe yourself like you're telling a friend what makes you interesting. Don't filter. Be weird. Be specific. Then cut it to 150 words and paste it into your profile. You'll be done before your tea goes cold.
