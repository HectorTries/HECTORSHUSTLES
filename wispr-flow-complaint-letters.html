I Got a £180 Refund in 9 Minutes — By Dictating My Complaint Instead of Typing It

17 October 2026 • Productivity

My broadband went down for six days last month. Six days of hotspot tethering, dropped work calls, and a support chatbot telling me to "try restarting my router." When the bill arrived — full price, obviously — I felt that familiar rage building. The kind that produces either a three-paragraph essay nobody reads or a furious rant that gets ignored.

I've written complaints before. They take 45 minutes minimum, because I rewrite every sentence to sound reasonable while I'm secretly furious. Too soft and they get filed away; too aggressive and they get dismissed.

This time, waiting for the kettle to boil, I decided to talk it out instead.

How it works for me

I opened Wispr Flow and gave myself one rule: explain it like I'm telling a mate what happened. I talked for five minutes — the outage dates, the three support calls with reference numbers, the work meeting I had to cancel, and what I actually wanted: a refund for the six dead days plus the extra mobile data I'd burned through.

That gave me around 350 words of raw material — dates, details, and one sentence that nailed it: "I've been a customer for three years and this is my first complaint, which should tell you how bad this week was."

At my laptop I spent four minutes trimming it — facts first, impact, one clear ask — and sent it through the complaints form. Total time: nine minutes.

Why it works so well

Complaints are stories, and stories come out better spoken. When I type, I self-censor — I soften the facts because anger feels rude on screen. When I talk, the facts just come out in order, with the frustration audible enough to keep me honest but not so raw that it lands on the page.

Speaking also captures details typing forgets. Narrating the week out loud, the second support call's reference number surfaced naturally in sequence — I'd never have recalled it staring at a blank form.

The numbers

Old approach: 45 minutes of drafting, usually abandoned, zero refunds claimed in three years. New approach: five minutes of talking, four minutes of trimming, one email sent the same morning. Reply within 48 hours: £180 credit — six days' service plus the data top-up. Nine minutes of effort for £180 back.

Try it today

Got a bill, a broken delivery, or a service that let you down? Open a voice note, hit dictate, and start with "Here's what happened…" Talk for four minutes — dates, what you were promised, what it cost you. Then trim it to facts, impact, and one clear ask.
