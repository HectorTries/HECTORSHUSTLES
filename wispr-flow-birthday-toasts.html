I Gave a Birthday Toast That Got a Standing Ovation — I Dictated It on the Bus

15 October 2026 • Productivity

My best mate turned thirty last Saturday, and I was on the hook for the toast. Forty people, a private room above the pub, and me — someone whose previous public speaking highlight was mumbling through a work standup. I'd been dreading it for three weeks.

Every typed draft sounded like a Hallmark card written by a committee. "On this momentous occasion…" Delete. "It is my distinct honour…" Delete. The night before the party, stuck on the bus home, I gave up typing and just started talking into my phone.

The next evening, people laughed, my mate's mum wiped her eyes, and someone actually shouted "speech!" when I sat down. I didn't tell anyone I wrote it on the number 43.

How it works for me

I opened Wispr Flow on the bus and gave myself one rule: no editing, just memories. I talked for five minutes straight — the disastrous camping trip where he set fire to a Pot Noodle, the 2am airport pickup he never mentions, the way he always orders dessert "for the table" and eats all of it.

That ramble gave me around 350 words of raw material — messy, specific, completely in my voice. One line basically wrote itself: "Dave collects people the way other people collect vinyl — carefully, and he never lets go of a good one."

Then at home I spent ten minutes shaping it: one funny story to open, one sincere bit in the middle, raise the glass to close. Two minutes to rehearse out loud. Done.

Why it works so well

Toasts are spoken, so typing them first is starting in the wrong medium. Typing makes you formal and stiff. Talking makes you sound like the friend everyone came to hear — because you are.

Speaking also digs up details you'd never type. I'd never have sat down and typed "the Pot Noodle fire of 2019." But I said it out loud because it was funny and true, and it got the biggest laugh of the night.

The numbers

Old approach: three weeks of dread, four deleted drafts, zero usable words. New approach: five minutes of talking on the bus, ten minutes of shaping, one toast that landed. Two guests asked me afterwards if I'd had help writing it. My mate still quotes the vinyl line back at me.

Try it tonight

Got a birthday coming up? Open a voice note, hit dictate, and finish the sentence "The funniest thing we've ever done together was…" Keep talking for three minutes. Don't edit, don't restart. You'll have everything a good toast needs.
