I Cleared 6 Overdue Invoices in 22 Minutes — By Dictating Them on a Walk

22 October 2026 • Productivity

I had £1,840 sitting in unpaid invoices. I knew exactly who owed what. I just hadn't sent the nudges.

Not because I'm disorganised. Because every invoice email feels awkward to type. Too stiff and I sound like a debt collector. Too friendly and it gets ignored for another month. So I opened a blank draft, rewrote the first line four times, closed the tab, and told myself "tomorrow." For three weeks.

Last Tuesday, on my evening walk, I dictated all six instead.

How it works for me

I opened Wispr Flow and talked through each invoice like I was briefing a friend. Client name, amount, date sent, and one honest line about the work. "Design retainer, £450, sent 12 September, homepage refresh they loved."

Two minutes per invoice, roughly. Six invoices in about twelve minutes of talking.

That gave me around 500 words of raw material — messy but complete. The line that made it work: "Just checking this one didn't get lost — here's the link again, no rush this week."

I spent ten minutes trimming the ramble into six short emails: one line of context, amount, due date, payment link. Polite, direct, done. All sent before I got home.

Why it works so well

Invoice chasing is a tone problem, and tone comes out better spoken. When I type, I overthink every word and sound either cold or apologetic. When I talk, I sound like myself — friendly, matter-of-fact, easy to say yes to.

Speaking also surfaces the details typing skips. Talking it through, I remembered to thank one client for the referral, to attach the updated bank details on another, and to waive a £25 late fee that wasn't worth the goodwill hit.

The numbers

Old approach: three weeks of avoidance, zero pounds chased, one awkward Sunday rewriting the same draft. New approach: twelve minutes of talking, ten minutes of trimming, six emails sent. Two clients paid within 48 hours — £780 back in my account. The rest replied with dates. Zero dread.

Try it today

Got an invoice you've been avoiding? Open a voice note, hit dictate, and start with "Here's who owes what and why…" Talk for two minutes per invoice — name, amount, one line about the work. Then trim it into context, amount, link.

If unpaid admin lives as a guilty swirl in your head, give Wispr Flow a go with my link — it's free to start, and it turned my £1,840 invoice pile into a 22-minute walk. Say the nudge, get paid.
