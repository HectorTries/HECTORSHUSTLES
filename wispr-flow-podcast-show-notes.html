Podcast Show Notes That Take 10 Minutes, Not an Hour

31 August 2026 • Productivity

Here's a confession most podcasters won't make: the show notes are the worst part of the job. Recording the episode? Fun. Editing? Tolerable. But sitting down afterwards to write a summary, timestamps, and a "key takeaways" section for a 45-minute episode? That's admin work with a microphone's worth of guilt attached.

I used to dread it so much that my show notes were either a single lazy line ("Great chat about money stuff, go listen!") or a two-hour slog of scrubbing through the audio to find the moments worth linking. Neither is good. One looks unprofessional; the other eats the whole afternoon.

Then I realised the notes were already written — I just hadn't dictated them yet.

Here's what changed:

  • I recap out loud, not on the page. The moment the recording stops, I dictate a rough summary into Wispr Flow while the conversation is still fresh: what we covered, the one spicy take, the story worth clipping. Two rambling minutes, and the raw material for the notes already exists. No scrubbing through audio, no re-listening to my own voice.
  • Timestamps find themselves. Instead of noting "that bit at 23:40", I dictate "the bit where we argued about budgeting apps" and search the transcript to grab the exact minute. Wispr turns my vague memory into a searchable record, so the detailed notes write themselves from a transcript instead of from my brain.
  • The notes finally match the episode's personality. When I type notes, I write in careful, dry prose. When I dictate them, they sound like the episode — casual, opinionated, with the same jokes. Listeners have actually said the notes read like a friend explaining why they should listen. That's the whole point of show notes.

The weirdest result: my episodes get more listens from the notes themselves. People search "budgeting apps worth it" and land on my timestamped section, then stay for the whole episode. The notes stopped being a chore and became a growth channel.

If you've got an episode sitting in your edits with notes still unwritten, try this: hit record in Wispr Flow, and just say what happened in the episode out loud — badly, rambling, in whatever order it comes out. Then tidy that into the notes. Worst case, it's faster than your old way. Best case, the notes actually sound like you.
