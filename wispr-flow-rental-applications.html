I Beat 40 Applicants to My Flat — By Dictating My Rental Application on the Walk Home

18 October 2026 • Productivity

The letting agent said it straight: forty enquiries in two days, viewings back-to-back, landlord deciding by Friday. I'd lost two flats already that month — both times my "I'll apply tonight" turned into the next morning, and both times the place was gone.

Rental applications are the worst kind of writing. You're selling yourself without sounding desperate, listing references and move-in dates and employment details, all into a tiny portal text box that times out if you think too long. I used to spend an hour per application, rewriting my intro three times.

This time, walking home from the viewing, I decided to talk it out instead.

How it works for me

I opened Wispr Flow and gave myself one rule: explain why I want the flat like I'm telling a mate. I talked for five minutes — what I do for work, why the location works, my three years with zero missed rent at my last place, that I don't smoke and have no pets, and that I can move on the landlord's exact date with references ready.

That gave me around 300 words of raw material — plus one sentence that nailed it: "I'm looking for somewhere to stay three-plus years, not somewhere to land for six months."

At home I spent five minutes trimming it — intro, facts, one clear close — and submitted it within the hour. Total time: ten minutes.

Why it works so well

Applications are pitches, and pitches come out better spoken. When I type, I sound like a form — stiff, generic, forgettable. When I talk, I sound like a person a landlord would actually want as a tenant.

Speaking also keeps it specific. Narrating the viewing out loud, I mentioned the quiet street and the home-office corner I'd already pictured — details that proved I'd actually been there and wanted this flat, not just any flat.

The numbers

Old approach: an hour of drafting, submitted next day, two flats lost in a month. New approach: five minutes of talking, five minutes of trimming, application in within the hour. Agent replied that evening: shortlisted, then accepted. Ten minutes of effort for a flat in a forty-applicant race.

Try it today

Flat-hunting, or know someone who is? After your next viewing, open a voice note, hit dictate, and start with "Here's why I want this place…" Talk for four minutes — who you are, why the flat fits, your rental history. Then trim it to intro, facts, and one clear close.

If stiff forms and slow drafts keep costing you places, give Wispr Flow a go with my link — it's free to start, and it turned an hour-long dread-task into a ten-minute job that won me my flat. Say why you fit, get the keys.
