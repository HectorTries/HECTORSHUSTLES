I Never Forget a Gift Idea Anymore — I Just Say It Out Loud

20 September 2026 • Productivity

Gift ideas always hit me at the worst possible moment.

I'm in the shower and remember my brother mentioning a book he'd love. I'm out for a run and realise exactly what my wife would want for her birthday. By the time I'm near my phone notes app, it's gone. Every December I end up panic-buying generic presents I know are forgettable.

Now I just say it. The second an idea lands, I tap Wispr Flow and talk for ten seconds.

"Gift for Dad — he kept going on about that Japanese whetstone for his kitchen knives, around forty quid, check Amazon. Don't get the cheap one, he notices that stuff."

Done. No unlocking apps, no typing with wet hands, no "I'll remember it later" lies. Wispr Flow turns the ramble into a clean note — who it's for, what it is, the details that actually matter when you're buying weeks later.

Here's why voice beats a gift list every time: gift ideas come with context. When you type, you write "whetstone for Dad" and two months later you have no idea which one, what price, or why. When you talk, you naturally include the good stuff — where you saw it, the thing he said, the budget in your head. That extra colour is the difference between a gift that lands and a gift that's... fine.

I've got a running note now. Every idea goes in the moment it appears — "Mum loved that candle in the Bath shop, fig scent, grab two." "Friend's kid is obsessed with dinosaurs, age six, book not toy." When a birthday or Christmas rolls around, I'm not starting from zero. I'm picking from a list my past self already did the thinking for.

The result speaks for itself. Last year I bought four out of five Christmas gifts in one calm afternoon, all things people had actually mentioned wanting. No frantic high-street dash on the 23rd. No duplicate presents. My sister literally asked if I'd hired someone to shop for me.

If you're someone who cares about giving good gifts but has the memory of a goldfish, try this: next time someone mentions something they love, don't type it. Say it.
