I Asked 3 People for References in 15 Minutes — Without the Awkward Typing Spiral

19 October 2026 • Productivity

I'd been putting it off for a week. New job, offer conditional on references, and I needed three people to vouch for me — a former manager, an old client, and a colleague from two jobs back.

In practice, every draft I typed sounded either grovelling or demanding. "Sorry to bother you, I know you're busy, totally fine if not, but…" — delete. "Hi, I need a reference by Friday" — too blunt, delete. Forty minutes across three evenings and I hadn't sent a single message.

Asking for references is the most awkward writing there is. You're asking busy people for unpaid labour on your deadline, and every sentence feels loaded. I kept overthinking the tone until the deadline started thinking for me.

So on a Saturday walk, I talked them out instead.

How it works for me

I opened Wispr Flow and dictated each message like I was explaining it to a friend. For my old manager: what the new role is, why I thought of her, exactly what they'd ask (one short form, ten minutes max), and the Friday deadline. Two minutes of talking per person.

That gave me around 150 words of raw material each — warm and direct. The line that made all three work: "They just need to hear what it was like working with me — nothing formal."

I spent three minutes per message trimming the ramble into a short ask — context, the specific favour, the deadline, one easy out. All three sent within fifteen minutes of getting home.

Why it works so well

Reference requests are favours, and favours come out better spoken. When I type, I hedge everything with five layers of apology. When I talk, I sound like myself — direct, grateful, normal.

Speaking also forces the specifics. Talking it through out loud, I naturally included the role title, the deadline, and exactly what they'd need to do — the three details that make saying yes easy and that my typed drafts kept burying under throat-clearing.

The numbers

Old approach: forty minutes over three evenings, zero messages sent, deadline looming. New approach: six minutes of talking, nine minutes of trimming, three personalised requests sent the same afternoon. All three said yes within 48 hours — the thing blocking my job offer, done in fifteen minutes.

Try it today

Need a reference, a favour, or an intro you've been avoiding? Open a voice note, hit dictate, and start with "Here's what I need and why I'm asking you…" Talk for two minutes — the context, the specific ask, the deadline. Then trim it to context, favour, deadline, and one graceful out.
