How I stop avoiding hard conversations

9 September 2026 • Productivity

I hate hard conversations.

I will put them off for days. I rewrite the same text six times. I sound too cold. Then too soft. Then I close the app.

I am a founder in London. I am building Cullify. I am posting daily on TikTok for Fortune and Folly. I am getting married soon. My life right now is full of people stuff. Feedback. Boundaries. Money.

Last month I had three awkward ones stacked up. A contractor invoice that was too high. A friend who owed me money from a trip. And a no I needed to say to extra work I did not have time for.

All small. All painful. All stuck in my head.

Here is what I do now. I talk it out with Wispr Flow.

I put my headphones on. I go for a walk. I just ramble for two minutes. I say what happened. I say what I want. I say what I am scared to sound like.

I do not try to sound smart. I just talk like I would to a mate.

Wispr Flow turns it into clean text. Then I tell it the tone I need. Firm but kind. Short and clear. No drama.

For the contractor I said: "Hey, thanks for the fast work. This invoice is higher than we agreed. Can we stick to the quoted price? Happy to talk if I missed something."

It took four minutes. I sent it. He replied in an hour. He agreed to the original price. No fight. Sorted.

For my friend I said: "Hey man, can you send the 120 for the flights when you get a sec? No rush this week but I want to close it out."

He sent it the same day. He even thanked me for reminding him. I had stressed about that one for two weeks.

For the extra work I said: "Thanks for thinking of me. I cannot take this on right now. My plate is full with my startup and wedding prep. Happy to point you to someone else."

Short. Clear. Done. I felt relief straight away.

That is the result I care about. Less dread. Faster replies. Relationships stay intact.

My process is simple.

  • Open Wispr Flow and talk for 1 to 2 minutes. Say the facts and what outcome you want.
  • Ask for a short draft in a firm but friendly tone.
  • Read it once. Cut one line. Send it.

I do this for feedback too. I say what is working. I say what needs to change. I ask for one clear next step. It keeps me from sounding harsh when I type while annoyed.

If you avoid hard chats like I did, try talking first and typing second. It is faster. It sounds more like you. And you stop carrying it around all week.
