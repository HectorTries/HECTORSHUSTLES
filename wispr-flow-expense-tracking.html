I Log Every Penny Without Opening a Spreadsheet

21 September 2026 • Productivity

I used to be terrible at tracking spending.

Not the big bills — those are automatic. It was the small stuff. The £4.50 coffee, the £12 lunch, the random Amazon order at 11pm. Each one felt too small to log, so I never did. Then the end of the month arrived and £300 had vanished somewhere I couldn't explain.

Budgeting apps never fixed it. Opening an app, finding the right category, typing in the amount — by step two I'd already given up. The friction was bigger than the purchase.

Now I just say it. The second I pay, I tap Wispr Flow and talk for five seconds.

“Coffee, four fifty, Pret near the office. Workday lunch category.”

“Taxi home, eighteen quid, split with Dan so really nine.”

Done. No app-hunting, no dropdown menus, no receipt photo that I'll never look at again. Wispr Flow turns the mumble into a clean line — amount, what it was, the context that matters when you review later.

Here's why voice works where budgeting apps fail: spending happens in motion. You're walking out of a shop, standing at a till, rushing for a train. Typing means stopping. Talking means you don't break stride. I capture things I'd never bother to log otherwise, and those small leaks are exactly where the money goes.

At the end of the week I dump it all in one place. I dictate a quick roundup — “okay, this week: coffees about twenty, lunches forty, that taxi, plus the Sainsbury's run at sixty-three” — and suddenly I have a real picture. No guilt, no detective work. Just facts.

The result surprised me. First month of doing this, I found £140 in repeat subscriptions and snack runs I was barely aware of. I cancelled two subscriptions on the spot. My “mystery spending” dropped to almost zero — not because I spent less on purpose, but because saying it out loud made me notice.

If your money keeps evaporating and spreadsheets make your eyes glaze over, try this: next time you pay for something, don't type it. Say it.
