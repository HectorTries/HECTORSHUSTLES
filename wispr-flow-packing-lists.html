I Packed for a Weekend Trip in 3 Minutes — By Talking to My Suitcase

3 October 2026 • Productivity

I used to pack the same way everyone does: the night before a trip, half-watching TV, throwing things in a bag and telling myself I'd remember the important stuff in the morning.

I never did. Last month it was my charger. Before that, contact lens case. Once I packed two left shoes. Not my finest hour.

This weekend was different. I stood in front of my open suitcase, opened Wispr Flow, and just talked through the trip.

The 3-minute suitcase scan

Here's roughly what I said: "Friday to Sunday, Manchester, cold. Jeans, grey jumper, white T-shirt, two pairs of socks. Trainers on my feet. Wash bag — toothbrush, deodorant, contact lenses and the case, don't forget the case. Laptop and charger. Book for the train. Coat with the hood, umbrella just in case."

No checklist app. No tapping through tiny boxes. No sitting at a desk writing a list I'd forget to look at. Just standing there, looking at the bed, saying what I needed as I saw the gaps.

Wispr Flow turned it into a clean packing list — every item separated, quantities intact. I glanced at it, packed straight from it, ticked nothing off because I didn't need to. Three minutes, bag zipped.

Why voice wins for packing

Packing is physical. You're moving between rooms — bedroom for clothes, bathroom for toiletries, desk for chargers. Every time you stop to type, you lose the thread. You walk back to the bedroom and forget why you went there.

Talking keeps you moving. See it, say it, it's on the list. I caught things a sit-down list would have missed — the umbrella only surfaced because I was staring out the window at grey clouds while dictating. A typed list made on the sofa would never have included it.

It works on the way back too. Noticed I wore one shirt twice? Dictated a note for next time: pack three tops, not two. My future self says thanks.

The result

Three minutes to build the list. Zero forgotten essentials on a two-night trip. No emergency Boots run for a toothbrush, no begging the hotel for a charger. First trip in years where I unpacked and thought: that's everything.

If your packing involves guesswork and a 10pm panic the night before, try talking instead of typing. Stand by the bag, say the trip out loud, and pack from a list that actually matches where you're going.
