I Stopped Typing Standup Updates. My Mornings Got Better.

15 September 2026 • Productivity

Every morning, same ritual: Slack opens, #standup channel stares at me, and I spend ten minutes pecking out "Yesterday I… Today I will… Blockers: none."

Ten minutes. For three lines. That's embarrassing to admit — but I know I'm not the only one. Async standups are supposed to save time, yet writing them feels like homework nobody reads.

So I stopped typing them. I started dictating instead.

The old way was broken

Here's what my standup-writing used to look like: stare at the channel, try to remember what I actually did yesterday, type a draft, delete half of it because it sounds like rambling, rewrite it, post something stiff that reads like a status report from 2004.

The problem was never the standup itself. It was the friction of translating thoughts into keystrokes. I know what I did yesterday. Getting it out through my fingers just… stalls.

What I do now

I open Wispr Flow, hit the hotkey, and just talk: "Yesterday finished the checkout bug fix, got the staging deploy out, reviewed Priya's PR on the auth refactor. Today I'm picking up the onboarding email sequence and pairing with Sam on the database migration at two. No blockers, though I'm waiting on design mockups for the settings page."

That's it. Fifteen seconds of talking. Wispr Flow cleans up the filler words, fixes the punctuation, and drops structured text straight into Slack. What lands in #standup looks like this:

Yesterday: Shipped checkout bug fix, staging deploy live, reviewed auth refactor PR.
Today: Onboarding email sequence + pairing on DB migration (2pm).
Blockers: Waiting on settings-page mockups from design.

Crisp. Scannable. Done before my coffee cools.

The surprising result

Two things changed that I didn't expect.

First, my updates got better. When I type, I self-edit into blandness — "worked on various tasks." When I talk, specifics come out naturally. Teammates actually started reacting to my standups because they contained real information.

Second, it killed the dread. Standup went from a ten-minute chore I procrastinated on to a thirty-second habit I do while the kettle boils. Over a month, that's roughly three hours handed back — for something my team finds more useful than before.

Try it tomorrow morning

Next standup, don't type. Talk it out with Wispr Flow — yesterday, today, blockers, fifteen seconds. Let it format the mess into something your team will actually read.

Your mornings will thank you.
