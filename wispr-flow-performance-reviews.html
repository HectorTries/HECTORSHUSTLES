I Wrote My Self-Assessment in 25 Minutes — By Bragging Out Loud First

11 October 2026 • Productivity

It's October, which means one thing at work: self-assessment season. That dreaded form where you rate yourself on twelve competencies, list your achievements, and somehow sound confident without sounding arrogant. Last year I spent three evenings on mine — most of it staring at the first question, typing "I believe I have demonstrated strong collaboration skills," and hating every word.

This year I dictated the whole thing in 25 minutes. And my manager said it was the best self-review she'd read from me.

How it works for me

I opened the review form, hit dictate on Wispr Flow, and answered each question the way I'd answer if a friend asked "so what did you actually do this year?" No corporate voice. Just talking: the project that nearly failed and how I saved it, the new starter I mentored, the process I fixed that nobody asked me to fix.

Fifteen minutes of talking gave me 800 raw words — specific, concrete, full of details I'd never have typed. Then I spent ten minutes tightening it up: trimming the rambling, keeping the stories. The corporate polish took minutes because the substance was already there.

Why it works so well

Self-assessments are hard to type because typing triggers your inner editor. Every sentence gets filtered through "does this sound professional enough?" before it hits the page, and the result is vague mush: "leveraged synergies," "drove alignment," "demonstrated ownership."

Talking bypasses all of that. When you speak, you tell stories — what happened, what you did, what changed. And stories are exactly what managers want to read. My manager literally highlighted two paragraphs and wrote "great examples — I'll use these in your review." Nobody has ever said that about "demonstrated ownership."

The numbers

Last year: 3 evenings, ~4 hours total, generic answers I was embarrassed to submit, and I forgot half my wins. This year: 25 minutes total (15 talking, 10 editing), specific examples for every competency, and a manager who quoted my own self-assessment back at me. Same year of work. Completely different story on paper.

Try it this review cycle

Open your self-assessment form, hit dictate, and answer the first question like you're telling a friend what you did this year. Don't filter. Brag a little. Then clean it up and move to the next question. You'll finish before your coffee goes cold.
