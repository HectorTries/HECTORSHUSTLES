I Drafted 9 Emails on the Train This Morning — Without Typing a Word

10 September 2026 • Productivity

My commute used to be email purgatory. Crammed on the 8:15, phone in one hand, coffee in the other, trying to thumb-type a reply while the train lurched through Clapham Junction. Autocorrect mangling names. Half-finished drafts abandoned by the time we hit Waterloo.

This morning I did it differently: 9 emails drafted before I got off the train. Zero typing.

Here's the setup. Phone in my pocket, earbuds in, Wispr Flow on my iPhone. I open an email, tap the mic, and just talk quietly: "Reply to James — sorry for the delay, budget approved, let's lock in Friday, ask him to send the invoice." Wispr drops a clean, punctuated draft straight into Gmail. I glance, hit send. Next one.

The bumpy-train test is the real test. Thumb-typing fails when you're standing, swaying, one-handed. Voice doesn't care. I dictated a three-paragraph quote follow-up wedged between two strangers, phone barely out of my pocket. Nobody blinked — everyone else is on calls anyway.

The numbers from this week: I tracked it. Commute email on typing days: 2–3 replies per 30-minute journey, most with typos I'd fix later. Commute email on voice days: 8–10 drafts per journey, sent or scheduled before I reach the office. That's basically my entire morning inbox, done before I sit down.

Why it works so well on mobile specifically:

  • No keyboard needed. Wispr handles punctuation, capitalisation, even email addresses from speech. "Send to sarah at cullify dot co dot uk" — nailed it.
  • Whisper mode for quiet carriages. The quiet coach used to be a no-email zone. Now I literally whisper and it still transcribes perfectly.
  • Think out loud, edit on glance. I say what I mean loosely, Wispr tightens it. One quick skim before sending catches everything.

The best part? I walk into the office with an empty inbox and a full coffee. My first hour used to be defensive email catch-up. Now it's deep work — scripts, Cullify builds, actual thinking.

If you commute, your train seat is an email superpower waiting to happen. Stop thumb-typing. Start talking.
