I Saved My Grandma's Recipe Without Touching My Phone Once

18 September 2026 • Productivity

Last Sunday my hands were covered in flour, my grandma was on speakerphone, and she was finally telling me how she makes her lemon drizzle cake.

Old me would have panicked — "hold on, let me wash my hands, find a pen" — and lost half the details. This time I just tapped Wispr Flow with my knuckle and talked.

"Okay. Gran's lemon drizzle. Two hundred grams butter, two hundred sugar, three eggs, zest of two lemons, two hundred self-raising flour. Bake one-eighty for forty minutes. Syrup: eighty grams sugar, juice of two lemons, pour over while warm. She says don't overmix, and use the glass dish not the tin."

Sixty seconds of rambling while kneading. Wispr Flow turned it into a clean recipe — ingredients listed, steps numbered, her little tips kept intact. I rinsed my hands, checked the screen, done. Nothing lost.

Here's why this works so well: cooking is the worst time to type. Your hands are messy, the timing matters, and the thought vanishes if you wait. Voice captures it in the moment. You're already talking to yourself in the kitchen anyway — "okay, what did she say, two lemons or three?" Talking lets you think while your hands keep working.

I've started using it for everything in the kitchen. Tweaks to my own chilli — "added extra cumin, less water, twenty minutes longer, turned out great." Shopping add-ons mid-cook — "we're out of olive oil, add eggs to the list." Last week I dictated a whole soup variation while stirring, and now I actually have it saved instead of forgetting it by dinner.

The numbers tell the story. Writing up that cake recipe by hand would have taken fifteen minutes plus rewashing up twice. Dictating it took one minute, mid-bake, zero interruption. That's not just convenience — that's the difference between keeping a family recipe and losing it.

If you cook at all, do yourself a favour: next time your hands are full and your brain has an idea, just say it. Let the mess stay on your hands, not in your notes.
