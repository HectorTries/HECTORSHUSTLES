The 10-minute dictation that plans my whole week

24 August 2026 • Productivity

Use case: Turning a Sunday-night brain dump into a clean weekly plan — without opening a single blank document.

Sunday night used to be my stress hour. I'd sit down to "plan the week," stare at an empty notes app, and end up scrolling instead. The problem wasn't organisation — it was the blank page. My week lived in my head, and there was no bridge between thinking it and writing it.

The bridge turned out to be my voice.

Now every Sunday I do what I call the 10-minute dump. I hit record on my phone and just talk for ten minutes. No structure, no filter. Everything that's rattling around — the project that's stuck, the email I keep avoiding, the thing I said I'd do three weeks ago, the idea that won't leave me alone. It all comes out, messy and fast, because talking is effortless in a way typing never is.

Then Wispr Flow does the thing that changed everything: it turns that ramble into clean, readable text while I'm still walking back from the kitchen.

Here's where the magic happens. Once it's text, I don't have to organise it — I just read it back and the priorities are obvious. The thing I talked about first and loudest is the thing that actually matters. The task I mentioned twice in passing is the one I've been avoiding. My own voice, on the page, tells me what my week should be.

Then I spend five minutes turning the dump into three lists:

  • The one thing — the single outcome that makes the week a win if nothing else gets done
  • The must-dos — three to five commitments, real ones, not wishes
  • The parked — everything else, safely captured so my brain can let go of it

Total time: fifteen minutes. And the week starts with a plan I actually believe in, because I made it out loud instead of forcing it onto a blank page.

The best part is the dumps compound. Three months from now I can scroll back and see what I was worried about in March that never happened, which ideas actually survived contact with reality, and how much more I committed to than I could do. It's a free personal retrospective.

If your weeks start with a vague sense of overwhelm and a notes app full of good intentions — try talking instead of typing. Ten minutes, out loud, and let the words land on the page for you.
