I Clear 50 Emails in 20 Minutes Now (Without Typing a Single One)

21 August 2026 • Productivity

Email is a black hole of your attention — unless you stop treating it like a typing task. Here is how I turned it into a voice task.

4 min read · Aug 21, 2026

There's a version of me that starts the day with 50 unread emails and ends it with... 50 unread emails plus a headache.

The problem was never the volume. It was the friction.

Every email you reply to is a tiny writing task. And your brain treats "opening a blank reply box" the same way it treats "write an essay" — it stalls, it drafts, it edits, it procrastinates. Multiply that by fifty and you've lost your entire morning to a task that should take minutes.

So I stopped typing. And it changed everything.

The 20-Minute Inbox Routine

Here's what my inbox routine looks like now:

1. Skim everything, reply to nothing (2 minutes). Open the app, scroll fast, flag the ones that actually need a reply. Most emails are noise — delete or archive them instantly. You're not answering everything, you're answering what matters.

2. Dictate your replies out loud (12 minutes). This is the part that used to take me an hour. Now I just... talk. I hold down the mic on Wispr Flow and say what I'd actually say if the person were standing in front of me:

"Hey Sam — yes, the invoice works, I've signed it and sent it back. One note: we're moving the kickoff to Thursday because the contractor pushed the build. Can you confirm that works on your end? Thanks!"

That's a complete, professional email in 15 seconds. Wispr Flow turns it into text, punctuation and all — while I'm still thinking about the content, not the mechanics of typing.

3. Glance, fix, send (6 minutes). Voice-to-text is fast, but you still scan each reply before hitting send — fix a name here, add a detail there, drop in the attachment link. Then send and move on.

Twenty minutes. Fifty emails. Done before your coffee's cold.

Why Voice Beats Typing for Email

It's not just speed. It's tone.

When you dictate an email, you naturally sound like a human. Your voice carries warmth, brevity, and personality. Typed emails — especially rushed ones — come out stiff and corporate. ("Please find attached..." — nobody talks like that.)

Dictating forces you to write the way you talk: short sentences, natural phrasing, zero bureaucratic filler. Recipients notice. Your replies get clearer, faster responses because they read like a person wrote them.

Plus there's the physical win: no more hunching over a laptop for an hour. You can pace. You can stand. You can do it while making coffee. Your neck and your inbox both thank you.

The One Rule That Makes It Work

Don't dictate a draft and then "fix it later." That's where voice workflows die — you end up typing anyway.

Instead: dictate the final answer. Say it like you mean it, in full sentences, exactly as you'd want it read. Then just skim for typos and send. The discipline of "speak it once, send it once" is what collapses your email time from an hour to twenty minutes.
