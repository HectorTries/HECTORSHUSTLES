Cold Emails That Don't Sound Like Everyone Else's

29 August 2026 • Productivity

Cold outreach has a tell: you can smell it from the first line. "I hope this email finds you well." "I'm reaching out because…" Every inbox is drowning in the same template, and everyone's learned to delete it on sight.

I used to write outreach the same way everyone does — careful, formal, safe. Then I realised the problem wasn't my offer, it was that the writing sounded like a robot doing an impression of a person.

So I stopped writing outreach emails. I started dictating them with Wispr Flow instead.

Here's what changed:

  • I talk to the person, not at them. When I dictate, I naturally write the way I'd actually open a conversation — "Hey, saw your post about X and it made me think of…" instead of "I am writing to you today regarding…". The email sounds like a human sent it, because a human did.
  • The first draft is the hard part, and I skip it. The reason outreach emails take an hour is that everyone tries to make draft one perfect. I dictate an ugly, honest version in two minutes, then spend ten making it clean. Same result, a fraction of the dread.
  • I reply faster, which means I win more. The people who reply to outreach are the ones who were quick AND human. Dictating means a response that used to sit in my drafts for half a day goes out the same morning.

The weirdest result: my reply rate went up. Not dramatically — but noticeably. I put it down to the tone. People can't tell you dictated it, but they can tell it doesn't read like spam.

If you send any kind of outreach — pitches, guest post ideas, partnership emails, even just "hey, let's grab a coffee" messages — try saying your next one out loud instead of typing it. Worst case, you save twenty minutes. Best case, it sounds like you.
