I Finally Do a Weekly Review — 15 Minutes, Just Talking

27 September 2026 • Productivity

Sunday nights used to give me that low-grade dread. Not because Monday was coming, but because I had no idea what I'd actually done all week. Tabs everywhere, half-finished tasks, that nagging feeling I'd forgotten something important.

I'd read about the GTD weekly review a dozen times. Sit down, process your inboxes, review your projects, plan the week. Every time I tried, I'd stare at a blank page for 40 minutes and give up. Typing it felt like homework.

Last Sunday I tried it with voice instead. I made a coffee, opened a blank note with Wispr Flow on, and just talked through my week.

"Okay. This week. Shipped the blog post on Tuesday, that did alright. Still haven't replied to Dan about the invoice — need to do that Monday morning. Gym twice, not three times. Felt scattered Thursday and Friday, too many context switches. Next week the big three are: invoice Dan, finish the landing page draft, and book the dentist. Everything else can wait."

Three minutes of honest rambling. Wispr Flow cleaned it up — no ums, proper paragraphs, the numbers right. I read it back and added three checkboxes at the top for next week's big three. Done.

Here's the part that surprised me: saying it out loud forced me to be honest. When you type a weekly review, it's easy to write vague stuff like "make progress on project." When you say it, you hear yourself being vague, so you correct yourself mid-sentence. "Make progress on — no, specifically, finish the first draft of the pricing section." The transcript captures the corrected version.

I've done it three weeks running now. Fifteen minutes tops: two minutes talking through what happened, two minutes on what's stuck, two minutes on next week's top three. The rest is sipping coffee and reading it back.

The payoff is Monday morning. Instead of spending the first hour figuring out what matters, I open last night's note. Three priorities, already decided by calmer Sunday-evening me. I just start.

It also catches the slow leaks. Week one I noticed I'd said "haven't replied to Dan" two weeks in a row. That stung enough that I finally sent the email Monday at 9am. A typed review would have let me gloss over it. Hearing myself say it twice? No hiding.

If your weeks blur together, try this tomorrow: open a note, turn on dictation, and answer three questions out loud. What got done? What's stuck? What are the top three for next week? Don't edit while you talk — just get it out.
