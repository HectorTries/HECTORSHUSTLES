I Wrote My Resignation Letter in 8 Minutes — Without Burning Bridges

13 October 2026 • Productivity

I'd been putting off my resignation letter for two weeks. Not because I wasn't sure about leaving — I was. I just couldn't face writing it. Every draft came out either too cold, too gushing, or like I was apologising for having a career.

Last Tuesday I dictated the whole thing in eight minutes while pacing around my kitchen. Sent it the same morning. My manager replied within an hour saying it was one of the most gracious resignations she'd read. We left on genuinely good terms.

How it works for me

I opened a blank note, hit dictate on Wispr Flow, and just talked it through like I was explaining it to a friend: why I'm leaving, what I've appreciated, what I want the handover to look like. No editing, no backspacing. Just "okay, first I want to thank her for the last two years, then the bit about the new role, then offer four weeks and a proper handover doc."

Three minutes of talking gave me about 250 words of raw material — warm, direct, actually sounding like me. Then I spent five minutes trimming it into a tight letter: thank you paragraph, news paragraph, handover offer, sign-off. Done before my coffee went cold.

Why it works so well

Resignation letters are hard to type because typing makes you overthink every word. Is "exciting opportunity" too smug? Is "grateful" enough? You end up with something stiff that sounds like a template from HR.

Talking forces honesty. When you speak, you say what you actually feel — "I've learned a lot here and I'm genuinely sad to go, but this new role is the right move." That's the tone you want: human, respectful, no drama. I caught one passive-aggressive line in my dictation about "limited growth opportunities" and cut it. Speaking it out loud made it obvious it didn't belong.

The numbers

Old approach: two weeks of avoidance, four abandoned drafts, one stressed Sunday night. New approach: eight minutes total, one sent letter, zero bridges burned. My manager offered to be a reference on the spot, and the handover took two short calls because I'd already talked through what mattered.

Try it tonight

Even if you're not quitting tomorrow, try this: open a blank note, hit dictate, and say out loud what you'd want your boss to hear if you left on the best possible terms. Thank them for one specific thing. State your news plainly. Offer a clean handover. You'll have a letter you're proud of in under ten minutes.
