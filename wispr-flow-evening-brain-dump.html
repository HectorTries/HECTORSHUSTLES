I Empty My Head Every Night in 3 Minutes — And Actually Sleep

5 October 2026 • Productivity

For years my brain refused to switch off at night. I'd get into bed and it would start: the email I forgot to reply to, the bill due Thursday, the weird idea for a side project, the thing I should've said in a meeting. I'd grab my phone to "quickly note it down" and wake up forty minutes later, eyes burning, having done everything except sleep.

I tried the notebook-on-the-nightstand thing. Lasted four days. Turning the lamp on, finding a pen, writing legibly half-asleep — it felt like homework. The notes were useless anyway: half-sentences I couldn't decode the next morning.

Last week I changed one thing. Now I sit on the edge of the bed, open Wispr Flow, and just talk for three minutes.

The 3-minute brain dump

Here's roughly what it sounds like: "Tomorrow — reply to James about the invoice, pay the water bill, gym at lunch if the knee feels okay. Also don't forget mum's birthday card, order it this week. Idea — that short video about voice notes for landlords, film it Wednesday. And I'm annoyed about the standup thing, just say it plainly tomorrow instead of overthinking it."

Messy. Rambling. Half worries, half to-dos, half random ideas. Exactly what my head sounds like at 11pm.

Wispr Flow turns it into a clean list — tasks separated from ideas, the worry reframed as one plain action. I glance at it, close the phone, and the loop in my head goes quiet. It's all captured. Nothing left to rehearse.

Why voice wins at bedtime

Typing at night is friction. The light, the keyboard, the urge to organise — it wakes you up instead of winding you down. And a blank notes page at 11pm feels like a test you're too tired to pass, so you skip it and keep ruminating instead.

Talking is the opposite. Eyes half-closed, voice low, no editing. You can admit the anxious stuff ("I'm annoyed about standup") alongside the boring stuff ("pay the water bill") without judging it. Saying it out loud shrinks it — most worries sound smaller once they leave your head.

The result

Seven nights in a row, asleep faster. No more 1am phone scrolling disguised as "being productive." Mornings start with a short list instead of a fog of half-remembered anxieties — I just open yesterday's dump and pick the first thing.

Three minutes of talking bought me back my evenings and my mornings.

If your brain runs meetings after lights-out, try dumping it out loud before bed. Say everything, trust the list, then sleep.
